## AI in your courses

Rutgers' [guidance on the use of AI](https://it.rutgers.edu/ai/guidance-on-the-use-of-ai-at-rutgers/) says AI tools "should not be considered permissible for coursework unless clearly stated or communicated by instructors." Rules vary by discipline, course, and instructor, and you are responsible for knowing the rules in each of your classes.

- Read the AI section of each syllabus. If there isn't one, ask your instructor before you use AI on graded work.
- "Allowed" usually has limits. An instructor might allow brainstorming or grammar checks but not AI-written answers or code.
- Under the [Academic Integrity Policy](https://policies.rutgers.edu/B.aspx?BookId=11914&PageId=459231), your work must be your own and, in OIT's words, "created without the aid of impermissible technologies, materials, or collaborations." Using AI where it isn't allowed can be an integrity violation.
- If you're unsure whether something counts, ask first. Your campus academic integrity office, such as [New Brunswick's](https://nbacademicintegrity.rutgers.edu/), can explain the process.

## Protecting your data

- Rutgers says confidential information, protected health information (PHI), and other proprietary Rutgers information "may not be appropriate for use in AI applications" ([OIT guidance](https://it.rutgers.edu/ai/guidance-on-the-use-of-ai-at-rutgers/)). Rutgers' [Information Classification Policy](https://policies.rutgers.edu/B.aspx?BookId=12018&PageId=459369) defines those categories.
- For Rutgers work, use the [Rutgers-provided tools](rutgers-tools.md) and sign in with your Rutgers account. That is when the university's data protections apply.
- Be especially careful with research data and with information about other students ([OIT, October 2026](https://it.rutgers.edu/2026/10/15/important-free-or-discounted-ai-offers-and-rutgers-data/)).
- In personal accounts, check each tool's privacy settings, including whether your chats are used to train its models.

## Checking what AI tells you

AI tools produce fluent, confident text that can be wrong. Treat their output as a draft from a well-read classmate who sometimes makes things up.

- Check facts, numbers, and quotes against a source you trust.
- Open every citation. AI tools invent papers, authors, and page numbers.
- Watch for one-sided or outdated answers, especially on current events and contested topics.
- The Libraries' [AI workshops](https://research.rutgers.edu/news/navigating-ai-rutgers-resources-and-workshops) include sessions on evaluating AI output.

## Citing AI

When AI use is allowed and you quote, paraphrase, or build on AI output, cite it and follow your instructor's disclosure rules. Save the conversation or its share link so you can show what you did.

| Style | Official guidance |
|:------|:------------------|
| APA | [Citing generative AI in APA Style](https://apastyle.apa.org/blog/cite-generative-ai-references) |
| MLA | [How do I cite generative AI in MLA style?](https://style.mla.org/citing-generative-ai-updated-revised/) |
| Chicago and others | The [Rutgers Libraries AI guide](https://libguides.rutgers.edu/artificial-intelligence) collects citation guidance for each style. |
