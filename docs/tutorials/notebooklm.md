# Gemini Notebook (NotebookLM)
{: .no_toc }

Gemini Notebook is Google's AI research and study assistant. You add your own sources, such as readings, slides, and notes, and it answers questions about them with citations back to the source. Google [renamed NotebookLM to Gemini Notebook](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/products/gemini-notebook/notebooklm-gemini-notebook/) in July 2026. It's free for Rutgers students with ScarletMail.

## 1. Sign in with ScarletMail

1. Go to [notebook.google.com](https://notebook.google.com/).
2. Sign in with your ScarletMail account, not a personal Gmail account. If you haven't set up ScarletMail, [activate it through ScarletApps](https://it.rutgers.edu/scarletapps/) first.

With ScarletMail, Rutgers says your uploads are not used to train Google's models ([Rutgers OIT](https://it.rutgers.edu/ai/gemini-notebook/)). For Google Workspace for Education accounts, Google says your files, chats, and outputs are not reviewed by humans and not used to improve its AI models ([Google](https://support.google.com/gemininotebook/answer/16337734)).

{: .warning }
Don't upload protected health information (PHI) or Rutgers business information. Only upload course material you're allowed to share. Some instructors don't allow their slides, problem sets, or exams to be uploaded to AI tools.

## 2. Create a notebook and add sources

Make one notebook per course or project. Each notebook only answers from its own sources.

Sources you can add ([full list](https://support.google.com/gemininotebook/answer/16215270)):

- PDFs, Word documents (.docx), PowerPoint files (.pptx), text, Markdown, and CSV files
- Google Docs, Slides, and Sheets from your Drive
- Public web pages and public YouTube videos with captions. Only the text or transcript is imported.
- Audio files, which are transcribed
- Pasted text

Each source can be up to 500,000 words or 200 MB. Copy-protected PDFs and paywalled pages won't import ([FAQ](https://support.google.com/gemininotebook/answer/16269187)).

## 3. Ask questions

Use the chat panel to ask about your sources. Answers include numbered citations. Click one to see the passage it came from, and check it, because summaries can still misread a source.

Questions that work well for studying:

- "Explain the main argument of the week 3 reading and the evidence for it."
- "What are the differences between these two lecture slides' definitions of X?"
- "Quiz me on chapter 4. Ask one question at a time and tell me what I got wrong."
- "Which topics come up in the lecture notes but not in the textbook chapter?"

Save answers you want to keep as notes.

## 4. Make study materials

The Studio panel turns your sources into ([overview](https://support.google.com/gemininotebook/answer/16206563)):

- **Study guides and reports**
- **Flashcards and quizzes**
- **Mind maps** of the topics in your sources
- **Audio Overviews**, podcast-style conversations about your material
- **Video Overviews**, slide decks, infographics, and data tables

## Limits

ScarletMail accounts follow Google's limits for school and work accounts, which are set by Rutgers' Google Workspace license. Rutgers lists up to 100 notebooks and 50 chat queries a day ([Rutgers OIT](https://it.rutgers.edu/ai/gemini-notebook/)). At that level, Google also caps each notebook at 50 sources and limits how many Audio Overviews, reports, flashcards, and other Studio outputs you can make per day ([Google's limits for school accounts](https://support.google.com/gemininotebook/answer/16337734)).

Personal Google accounts have different, compute-based limits, so tips written for personal accounts may not match what you see.
