# Rutgers tools
{: .no_toc }

What Rutgers licenses, runs, or teaches. Rutgers' Office of Information Technology (OIT) keeps the official list at [AI at Rutgers](https://it.rutgers.edu/ai/). For Rutgers work, OIT says to use only Rutgers-approved AI tools.

## Free with your Rutgers account

OIT says these "centrally funded tools are available to Rutgers students, faculty, and staff at no additional cost" ([AI at Rutgers](https://it.rutgers.edu/ai/)).

| Tool | What it does | How to sign in | Data notes |
|:-----|:-------------|:---------------|:-----------|
| [Microsoft Copilot Chat](https://it.rutgers.edu/ai/microsoft-copilot/) | General assistant for questions, research, summaries, drafting, code, and images. It isn't connected to Word, Outlook, or other Microsoft 365 apps. | Go to [copilot.cloud.microsoft](https://copilot.cloud.microsoft/) and log in with your Rutgers email and NetID password. | With a Rutgers account, your data is not used to train Copilot's models. Don't enter PHI or other confidential Rutgers information. |
| [Google Gemini](https://it.rutgers.edu/ai/google-gemini/) | Writing, revising, summarizing, brainstorming, and planning. LearnLM can build a personalized learning plan. Doesn't include Google AI Pro features such as Gmail and Docs analysis. | Go to [gemini.google.com](https://gemini.google.com/) and sign in with ScarletMail. If you don't have ScarletMail yet, [activate it through ScarletApps](https://it.rutgers.edu/scarletapps/). | Chats from ScarletMail accounts are not used to train Gemini. Don't enter PHI or other confidential Rutgers information. |
| [Gemini Notebook](https://it.rutgers.edu/ai/gemini-notebook/) (formerly NotebookLM) | Answers questions from the sources you upload (readings, slides, notes, web pages, videos), with citations. Also makes study guides, flashcards, quizzes, and audio overviews. | Go to [notebook.google.com](https://notebook.google.com/) and sign in with ScarletMail. See the [Gemini Notebook tutorial](tutorials/notebooklm.md). | ScarletMail uploads are not used for training. Don't upload PHI or Rutgers business information. |

{: .note }
Use your Rutgers account, not a personal one, for anything connected to your coursework or Rutgers work. The data protections above apply only to Rutgers accounts.

## Paid subscriptions through a department

Students can't buy these on their own. A faculty or staff member buys them through the University Software Portal, and some can then be assigned to students. OIT suggests the free tools above unless a unit has a specific need.

| Tool | Price (per user) | Notes |
|:-----|:-----------------|:------|
| [Microsoft 365 Copilot](https://it.rutgers.edu/ai/microsoft-365-copilot/) | $20/month | Copilot inside Word, Excel, PowerPoint, Outlook, and Teams. |
| [Google AI Pro](https://it.rutgers.edu/ai/google-ai-pro/) | $20/month | Gemini inside Gmail, Docs, and Drive. |
| [ChatGPT Edu](https://it.rutgers.edu/ai/chatgpt-edu/) | From $10/month | ChatGPT with custom GPTs under university data protections. |

## Research computing

[Amarel](https://it.rutgers.edu/research-computing/amarel/) is Rutgers' shared computing cluster for research. Access is free and first-come, first-served for all Rutgers researchers, including students. Free jobs can be preempted by jobs from groups that own nodes. The Amarel page doesn't say whether free accounts can use its GPU nodes. If you need GPUs for research, ask your advisor or lab. To get an account, use the [Amarel access request form](https://it.rutgers.edu/research-computing/amarel-cluster-access-request/) (NetID login required).

For free GPUs for coursework and small projects, see [Coding and data](coding.md#free-gpus-and-notebooks).

## Workshops, guides, and campus programs

- [Rutgers University Libraries AI workshops](https://research.rutgers.edu/news/navigating-ai-rutgers-resources-and-workshops) are free for all students. Fall 2026 topics include the ethics of generative AI, evaluating AI output, managing references, and using AI in literature reviews. Register on the [Libraries events calendar](https://www.libraries.rutgers.edu/events).
- The [Libraries Artificial Intelligence guide](https://libguides.rutgers.edu/artificial-intelligence) covers how generative AI works, prompt writing, AI in research and publishing, and citing AI.
- The [AI features in library databases guide](https://libguides.rutgers.edu/ai-eresource-platforms) explains the AI tools built into Scopus, Web of Science, JSTOR, ProQuest, and other platforms the Libraries license.
- [Critical AI @ Rutgers](https://sites.rutgers.edu/critical-ai/) runs events and publishes a [guide to critical AI literacies for students](https://criticalai.org/2026/01/29/student-guide-to-critical-ai-literacies/).
- The [Rutgers-Camden AI Campus](https://camden.rutgers.edu/research/research-office/ai-campus) runs AI project cohorts and talks.
- [AI innovation at Rutgers](https://it.rutgers.edu/ai/ai-innovation-initiatives/) lists the university's AI research centers and labs, which is useful if you're looking for research opportunities.
- [School of Arts and Sciences IT: Using AI at Rutgers](https://sas-it.rutgers.edu/artificial-intelligence) is a short summary of expectations and approved tools.
